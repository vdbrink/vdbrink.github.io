# Mossarium
*A small living ecosystem on my desk*

## Introduction

While creating my [Pinterest mood board](/desk/office_mood_board) for my home office, I found a lot of inspiration for moss terrariums, also known as mossariums.
You will also see them called a mini rainforest, a micro landscape, a miniature ecosystem, a moss garden or a jar garden.
I wanted a small piece of nature on my desk, but without the care of a normal houseplant.

Buying a fully decorated one was quite expensive, but I found a DIY kit that made it possible to build my own for a reasonable price.
Later, my local garden center and online they started selling complete mossariums, so it is worth checking there too.

Now it stands on my desk in my home office, next to my monitor. It looks amazing, is compact and it gives a piece or rest to look at during the day.

## How it works

A mossarium is a small, mostly closed ecosystem. The plants and the moss inside create their own climate:

* **Water cycle:** the water in the soil evaporates, condenses on the glass and drips back into the soil. So the same water is used over and over again.
* **Photosynthesis:** with light, the plants and the moss make oxygen and take in carbon dioxide.
* **Recycling:** fallen leaves are broken down by tiny organisms in the soil and become nutrients for the plants.

<br>
Because of this, you need very little water. A well-balanced closed mossarium can stay closed for months, or even years, and only needs a bit of water and cut some grown plants now and then.
It's a special little ecosystem, which is also why it's so fun to watch it grow.

## Make one yourself

<div style="float:right; width:220px; margin-left:15px;">
<a href="/desk/images/mossarium_creation.jpg">
<img src="/desk/images/mossarium_creation.jpg" alt="Mossarium during creation" width="100%" />
</a>
<em style="display:block; text-align:center">my mossarium during creation</em>
</div>

It is easy to make one yourself. You need a container, a few layers of material, plants and moss.

The layers, from bottom to top:

1. **Drainage layer:** 2-3 cm of small stones or expanded clay balls, where the extra water can collect.
2. **Separation layer:** a piece of mesh or a layer of sphagnum moss, so the soil doesn't mix with the drainage.
3. **Activated charcoal:** a thin layer to keep the water fresh and avoid bad smells and mold.
4. **Substrate:** 3-5 cm of terrarium or potting soil without extra fertilizer. Make it a bit higher at the back for depth.
5. **Deco wood:** place a piece of deco wood (driftwood) on the substrate first, because it gives your mossarium structure and height. Rinse it before use, and look at a local aquarium store for the wood you like the most.
6. **Plants:** place the plants with the tallest ones at the back. Use long tweezers in a narrow container.
7. **Moss:** place the moss pieces between the plants and on the open spots, and press them down lightly.
8. **Decoration:** add stones or small figures, like my frogs and deco door. Don't use too much, because the container is small.

Finish with adding a bit of water, close the lid and place it in bright, indirect light.
Not in direct sun, because the glass can heat up and burn the plants.

## Moss types

Different moss types create different shapes and textures. Combining two or three types looks more natural than one flat layer.

You can also look in your own garden and neighborhood for different kinds of moss, for example on old walls, stones, tree trunks or between the paving stones.
Take only a small piece, and only where it is allowed. Remove loose dirt, insects and slugs before you place it in your mossarium.

## Maintenance

A mossarium needs little care, but there are a few things to watch.

### Water

* Check the glass: a light mist for part of the day means the humidity is right.
* If the glass is wet all day and water runs down, open the lid for a while to let the moisture escape.
* If the moss looks dry or pale, add a little water with a spray bottle. Use clean water (distilled or rainwater is best) and stop before the soil gets muddy.
* It is easier to add a bit more water later than to remove too much from a closed container.

### Cutting the plants

* Trim plants that grow against the glass or over the moss, with a small pair of scissors.
* Remove dead or brown leaves with tweezers, to avoid mold.
* Wipe the inside of the glass when the view gets poor.
* A little mold can appear in a new mossarium. Remove it, and ventilate a few days.

### Required tools

* Long tweezers (stainless steel) to place and remove plants and moss
* Small scissors to trim the plants
* A spray bottle for water
* A soft brush or cloth to clean the glass
* A small spoon to add soil
